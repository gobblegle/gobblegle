Paul Nation's BNC/COCA word family lists, version 1.3.0
=======================================================

basewrd1.txt ... basewrd35.txt are the complete, unchanged files from
basewords_130.zip ("BNC/COCA basewords ver. 1.3.0"), downloaded 2026-11-27 from
Laurence Anthony's Word Family Finder:
  https://laurenceanthony.net/software/wordfamilyfinder/
  (file: https://laurenceanthony.net/software/wordfamilyfinder/downloads/basewords_130.zip,
   last changed there 2025-01-04)
Paul Nation's own vocabulary page sends readers there for the full lists:
  https://www.wgtn.ac.nz/lals/resources/paul-nations-resources/vocabulary-lists

What the files hold
  basewrd1-30   word families, most common first (1st 1000, 2nd 1000, ...)
  basewrd31     proper names
  basewrd32     marginal words (exclamations, swear words, letters)
  basewrd33     transparent compounds (RAINFALL, THUNDERSTORM)
  basewrd34     acronyms
  basewrd35     words with accents
  Each family: the head word at the start of a line, then its members on the
  lines below it, indented with a tab. The number after each word is a count.

Information-on-the-BNC_COCA-word-family-lists-20180705.pdf is Nation's own
description of the lists (revised 5 June 2018), from his "Vocabulary analysis
programs" page. It gives this way to cite the lists (the files here are
version 1.3.0):
  Nation, I.S.P. (2017). The BNC/COCA Level 6 word family lists (Version 1.0.0)
  [Data file]. Available from http://www.victoria.ac.nz/lals/staff/paul-nation.aspx

License: neither website, the zip, nor the PDF states a license or permission
to share copies of the lists. Nation's and Anthony's sites offer them as free
downloads, and the PDF encourages people to make their own lists.

The Gobblegle board editor (editor.html) reads these files when you click a
word in a word list. It combines them with the English Speller Database
(esdb/): any two families that share a word, from either list, are joined
into one list. The popup shows that combined list, keeping only words that
are in the game dictionary (in_all_three_333k.txt), and credits both sources.
